Document Layout & Mechanics

Standard visual architecture for papers submitted in MLA 9th Edition format.

Standard Document Settings

Element MLA Standard Requirement Common Mistake to Avoid
Margins 1 inch on all four sides (top, bottom, left, right). Using default 1.25" margins or custom narrow margins.
Spacing Double-spaced ($2.0$) throughout entire document. Adding extra line breaks (Enter key) between paragraphs.
Font Choice Legible font (Times New Roman 12 pt, Arial 11 pt, Calibri 11 pt). Mixing multiple font styles or sizes across headings.
Paragraph Indent $\frac{1}{2}$ inch ($0.5"$) indent on the first line of each paragraph. Using spaces instead of the Tab key or ruler margin setting.

In-Text Citations (Author-Page)

How to correctly integrate and attribute source claims within your prose.

Citation Formulas

1. Standard Parenthetical Citation

"Direct quote or paraphrase" (Author Lastname PageNumber).

Example: "State preemption acts as an absolute bar to local legislation" (Cabrera 45).

2. Signal Phrase Citation (Author Named in Text)

According to Author Lastname, "Direct quote or paraphrase" (PageNumber).

Example: Cabrera states that local municipal mandates often conflict with state policy (45).

Handling Special Citation Cases

If an online article or website lacks page numbers, omit numbers entirely. Never make up page or paragraph numbers.

Example: As state policy reports confirm, local laws are constrained by preemption (Cabrera).

Use a shortened version of the title in quotation marks (for articles) or italics (for books/websites).

Example: Municipalities are increasingly limited by legislative limits ("Understanding Local Authority" 12).

Replace page numbers with a specific timestamp or time range formatted as hh:mm:ss.

Example: Conflict between city councils and state policy is expanding (Alexander 00:14:22-25).

Block Quotations (4+ Lines of Prose)

When quoting prose longer than 4 typed lines, set it off as a block quote:

  • Indent the entire block 0.5 inches ($\frac{1}{2}"$) from the left margin.
  • Do not use quotation marks around the block text.
  • Place the parenthetical citation after the closing period (unlike standard quotes).

Municipal authority remains heavily constrained by state-level preemption laws across several domains:

State preemption acts as an absolute bar to local legislation, creating significant friction between city councils and state legislatures. When state statutory provisions explicitly overlap local mandates, local ordinances are consistently rendered unenforceable by judicial review. (Cabrera 112)

This dynamic demonstrates how state power overrides local control.

Works Cited: Core Elements Architecture

The 9 Container Elements used to construct any entry in MLA 9th Edition.

The 9 Core Elements Template

In MLA 9th Edition, every source entry is built using this precise sequence of elements and punctuation marks:

  1. Author. (First author listed as `Lastname, Firstname.`)
  2. "Title of Source." (Articles/chapters in quotes; books/websites in italics)
  3. Title of Container, (Larger work hosting the source: journal, website, book)
  4. Other contributors, (Translated by, edited by...)
  5. Version, (vol. 2, 9th ed., revised ed.)
  6. Number, (no. 4, issue 2)
  7. Publisher, (Omit if identical to container website title)
  8. Publication date, (19 Sept. 2026 or 2026)
  9. Location. (Page range `pp. 45-50`, URL, or DOI)

9 Core Container Elements Breakdown

# Element Formatting Style Followed By Example
1 Author Lastname, Firstname. Period Cabrera, Justin.
2 Title of Source "Quotes" (article) or Italics (book) Period "State Preemption Laws."
3 Title of Container Italics Comma Journal of Legal Studies,
4 Contributor Edited by / Translated by Comma edited by Camille Alexander,
5 Version vol. / 9th ed. / rev. ed. Comma 9th ed.,
6 Number vol. X, no. Y Comma vol. 14, no. 2,
7 Publisher Publisher Name (Omit "Co.", "Inc.") Comma Academic Press,
8 Publication Date Day Month Year or Year Comma 19 Sept. 2026,
9 Location pp. XX-YY / DOI / URL Period pp. 110-125.

Container 2 (Library Databases & Platforms)

When a source is found inside an online database (JSTOR, EBSCOhost, Canvas, ProQuest), it has two containers:

Author. "Source Title." Container 1 (Journal Title), vol. X, no. Y, Year, pp. XX-YY. Container 2 (Database Name), DOI or URL.
Real-World Example:
Alexander, Camille. "Evaluating Preemption Trends." Journal of Legal Studies, vol. 14, no. 2, 2024, pp. 110-125. JSTOR, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2012-7.

Access Dates Rule (Modern MLA standard)

Instructor Insight: In modern MLA 9th Edition, including an Accessed 19 Sept. 2026. date at the end of a web citation is optional. It is only required if the source has no publication/revision date or lacks a stable URL/DOI.

Works Cited Entry Blueprints

Copy/paste ready formulas and samples for common source formats.

Book (Single Author)

Lastname, Firstname. Title of Book. Publisher, Year of Publication.

Example:

Cabrera, Justin. Federalism and Local Power in Nevada Governance. Academic Press, 2025.

Scholarly Journal Article (With DOI)

Lastname, Firstname. "Title of Article." Journal Title, vol. X, no. Y, Year, pp. XX-YY. Database/Location, https://doi.org/XX.XXX...

Example:

Alexander, Camille. "Evaluating Preemption Trends in Modern States." Journal of Legal Studies, vol. 14, no. 2, 2024, pp. 110-125. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2012-7.

Webpage / Online Article

Lastname, Firstname. "Title of Webpage." Title of Website, Publisher/Sponsor, Day Month Year, URL.

Example:

Smith, Jordan. "Understanding Nevada Municipal Laws." Nevada Legal Review, 12 Aug. 2026, www.nvlegalreview.org/articles/municipal-laws.
